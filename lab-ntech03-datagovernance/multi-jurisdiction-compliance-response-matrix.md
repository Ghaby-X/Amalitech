# Multi-Jurisdiction Compliance Response Matrix

**Company:** Isoko Marketplace (Rwanda-based e-commerce platform serving customers in Rwanda, the EU, and the United States)

**Scenario:** Global customer data deletion requests

**Regulations:** Rwanda's Law N°058/2021 of 13/10/2021 Relating to the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy, GDPR, CCPA/CPRA

**Note on sourcing:** Rwanda-specific figures below (exact response-day counts, fine amounts) are not quoted from the primary legal text, since only a reference/summary page was available at the time of writing. Where a specific number isn't confirmed, it's flagged as an assumption rather than presented as a verified statutory fact.

---

## 1. Customer A: Diane Uwase (Rwanda)

### Legal Rights Analysis

- Diane has a right to request erasure of her personal data under Law N°058/2021, which mirrors the GDPR's data subject rights framework (access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction).
- Like most erasure rights, this is not absolute: it yields to a controller's separate legal obligations (e.g., tax recordkeeping) and to processing needed to establish or defend legal claims.

### Company Obligations

- Assess the request and delete personal data where no conflicting legal obligation exists.
- Retain only what Rwandan tax and company law require, and only for as long as required.

### Data Retention

- **Can retain:** Transaction and payment records needed for tax and audit compliance.
- **Retention period:** Governed by Rwandan tax and company-law recordkeeping requirements; the exact statutory period should be confirmed against Rwanda Revenue Authority rules rather than assumed.

### Response Deadline

- Law N°058/2021 does not appear (from available reference material) to fix an explicit day count for erasure requests the way GDPR does. Absent a confirmed statutory figure, aligning with the GDPR-style 30-day benchmark is a reasonable operating practice, pending verification against the primary text and any guidance from the Rwanda Data Protection and Privacy Office.

### Action Steps

- Verify identity.
- Delete personal/profile data not subject to a retention obligation.
- Retain only the minimum financial records required, tagged with a retention-expiry date.
- Log the request and its resolution for audit purposes.

### Draft Response

> "Dear Diane, your request has been received and processed. Your personal account data has been deleted, except for records we are legally required to retain for tax and compliance purposes."

## 2. Customer B: Sophie Laurent (France, GDPR)

### Legal Rights Analysis

- Sophie has a strong right to erasure under GDPR Article 17 ("Right to be Forgotten").

### Exemptions

- Data may be retained for:
  - Legal compliance (e.g., tax records)
  - Defense of legal claims
  - Freedom of expression and information, where applicable

### Company Obligations

- Delete personal data without undue delay.
- Inform any third-party processors who received the data.
- Ensure the deletion covers all systems, including backups where feasible.

### Response Deadline

- One month (30 days), extendable by a further two months for complex requests with notice to the data subject (GDPR Article 12(3)).

### Penalties for Missing Deadline

- Fines up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher.

### Action Steps

- Verify identity.
- Delete personal data across all systems.
- Notify third-party processors.
- Confirm deletion to the user in writing.

### Draft Response

> "Dear Sophie, we confirm that your personal data has been erased in accordance with GDPR Article 17. Any records we are required to retain by law have been securely preserved as permitted."

## 3. Customer C: Jordan Mitchell (California, CCPA/CPRA)

### Legal Rights Analysis

- Jordan has:
  - **Right to deletion**
  - **Right to opt-out of sale/sharing of personal information**

### Can Deletion Be Immediate?

- **No.** Jordan has an open chargeback dispute on a recent order, so records tied to that transaction must be retained until the dispute is resolved. This falls under CCPA's exemption for data needed "to complete the transaction for which the personal information was collected" and to comply with related legal obligations, not a general business convenience exemption.

### Company Obligations

- Pause deletion of transaction-related data until the dispute concludes.
- Immediately honor the opt-out of sale/sharing request regardless of the pending dispute, since that right is independent of the deletion timeline.

### Response Deadline

- 45 days, extendable to 90 days with notice to the consumer.

### Required Disclosures

- What data is collected.
- How it is used and shared.
- Confirmation that the opt-out has been implemented.

### Action Steps

- Verify identity.
- Stop selling/sharing personal data immediately.
- Inform the user that deletion of dispute-related records will follow once the dispute closes.
- Complete deletion once the retention obligation ends.

### Draft Response

> "Dear Jordan, we have processed your request to stop the sale/sharing of your personal information effective immediately. Records related to your open dispute will be deleted once that matter is resolved, as permitted under applicable law."

## 5. Key Insights

- **GDPR** remains the strictest in both clarity of timelines and severity of penalties.
- **CCPA/CPRA** centers on consumer control through opt-out rights rather than an opt-in consent model.
- **Rwanda's Law N°058/2021** follows GDPR's structure closely, but public reference material doesn't make its procedural deadlines and penalty schedule as easy to pin down without the primary text, so those specifics need direct verification rather than assumption.
- None of these three customers is shown as subject to more than one regime at once, but a real global company should plan for the overlap case (e.g., an EU citizen residing in Rwanda), where the strictest applicable rule should generally govern.

## Conclusion

Isoko Marketplace must handle each deletion request according to the customer's jurisdiction, balancing individual rights against retention obligations and active disputes. Because the company operates across Rwanda, the EU, and the US, it also needs a cross-border transfer safeguard for any data that moves between those regions, not just a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction deletion policy. Verified identity checks, minimal necessary retention, and clear, honest communication with the customer are what keep each response defensible under its governing law.
